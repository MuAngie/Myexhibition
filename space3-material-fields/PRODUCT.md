# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary viewer is the person who visited the exhibition and wants to revisit its spatial memory on a desktop computer. The experience should also be legible to someone who did not attend, without requiring prior knowledge of 3D controls.

## Product Purpose

Reconstruct four photo-documented exhibition spaces as a quiet, navigable HTML experience. Success means the viewer recognizes the sequence, scale, material presence, white-wall and black-ceiling contrast, lighting rhythm, partitions, columns, plinths, barriers, and characteristic installations. It is an atmospheric reconstruction, not a measured architectural survey.

## Brand Personality

Open, material, quiet. The interface is restrained and observational; the spatial scene carries the expression.

## Anti-references

Avoid futuristic virtual galleries, theatrical game interfaces, glossy glass panels, saturated ambient lighting, generic luxury styling, and claims of survey-level accuracy. Do not let navigation or artwork popups dominate the spatial experience.

## Design Principles

1. Space before interface: geometry, sight lines, and light define the experience.
2. Material evidence matters: grey mineral floors, warm white walls, black ceiling structure, and focused light remain distinct.
3. Quiet transitions: moving between zones should feel like turning a corner, not entering a game level.
4. Honest reconstruction: preserve clear photographic evidence and label uncertain connections as intentional approximations.
5. Representative display: artworks provide scale and rhythm in this phase, without enlarged-image viewing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Desktop-first keyboard and mouse controls, readable contrast, concise control guidance, and reduced-motion support. The experience must remain usable through visible zone buttons if free navigation is uncomfortable.
